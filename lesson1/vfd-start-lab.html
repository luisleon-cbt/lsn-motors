Safety on the real trainer

  • The instructor energizes and de-energizes the trainer. You measure and record.
  • Lockout/tagout before any wiring change, including moving the motor leads to the VFD. Verify zero voltage before you touch a terminal.
  • The shaft and coupling guard stays in place. Keep hands, sleeves, and badges away from the shaft and the belt.
  • Clamp the meter around one motor lead only, with the jaw fully closed, and keep your hands behind the guard on the meter.

About the numbers on this page

The numbers come from a simplified teaching model of the motor (it leaves out the stator resistance and other losses), so they agree with the rest of this lesson. Real motors draw roughly 5 to 7 times full-load current at start, and the first half cycle can be even higher.

The model gives a starting torque of 0.96 × rated. The trainer brake builds from 60 % of its set torque at standstill to the full setting at 150 RPM, so a fully loaded start does not stall in the model.

The VFD keeps volts per hertz constant (208 V at 60 Hz, 104 V at 30 Hz) and limits the current to 1.5 × FLA by holding the ramp. On the board the shaft and belt turn at 1/15 of the real speed so your eye can follow them. The tachometer and clamp meter show small reading noise, like real meters.

Click any part on the board to learn what it does.

Things to notice

  • Across the line, the current stays near the inrush value for most of the start and falls only when the rotor gets close to full speed.
  • Torque peaks (pull-out, 2.5 × rated) near 1440 RPM, late in the start, just before the current drops.
  • On the VFD the slip speed stays about 75 RPM at rated load at every frequency, so percent slip grows as the frequency drops: 4.17 % at 60 Hz, 8.3 % at 30 Hz.
  • The VFD start draws close to FLA because the field never runs far ahead of the rotor.
  • A clamp around all three leads reads about zero: the three currents cancel. Clamp one lead.
