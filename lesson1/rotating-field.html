How to use this lab

  1. Press Step to see the motor at standstill. Read the narration in the box below the board. It explains each state with real numbers.
  2. Press Step again for Accelerating and then Pull-out. On the board above, watch the rotor's purple mark catch up with the green field arrows. On the torque chart below, the green dot climbs to the top of the curve.
  3. Press Step for Full speed and then Reverse. In the Three-state matrix below, the gold column is the state the motor is in now. You are done with the narrated run when step 5 of 5 shows.
  4. Now run the motor yourself. Press Start motor and watch it speed up until it settles. Then pick a new Load on the shaft. Watch the speed, slip, and torque change in Live readings. You can also drag the Rotor speed slider to hold the rotor at any speed.
  5. Change Poles (P) or Supply frequency (f) to get a new synchronous speed (Ns). Press Swap two leads to reverse the field and the motor.
  6. Click any part on the board above, or a button under Parts of the motor, to learn what it does. Press Reset to start over.

Things to notice

  • The field always turns at Ns. Only the rotor speed changes, and the gap between them (the slip speed) decides everything else.
  • E2 = s × E20 and fr = s × f, so at full load the rotor sees only about 4% of its standstill voltage, and 2.5 Hz instead of 60 Hz.
  • Starting current is high because the slip is 1, but starting torque is only modest because the rotor power factor is poor.
  • Maximum torque (pull-out) comes at 20% slip in this model, where the rotor reactance equals the rotor resistance.
  • More load means more slip: raise the load and watch the operating point move to a lower speed on the torque chart.
  • Swapping any two leads reverses the field, and the rotor follows it the other way.

About these numbers

The numbers come from a simplified teaching model, the same one every activity in this lesson uses. Per-unit rotor values are R2 = 0.04 and X20 = 0.20 at E20 = 1 with constant flux, plus a magnetizing current of 0.4 × FLA. The model leaves out the stator resistance and other losses, and it gives every motor a rated slip of 4.17% (real nameplates vary: the F-3 fan slips 5.0%).

Real motors draw roughly 5 to 7 times full-load current at start. This model shows about 4.4 times, so read its starting current as "several times FLA", not as an exact value.

The load in this lab grows with speed, like a fan or a compressor, so it reaches the chosen value at full speed. The picture turns 100 times slower than the real motor so you can follow it.
